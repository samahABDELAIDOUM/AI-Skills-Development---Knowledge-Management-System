# AI Skills Development & Knowledge Management System

> **Purpose:** Build a reliable record of what I learn, how I practise,
> what I can do independently, and the evidence that proves it.

This repository is both my **personal learning notebook** and the
foundation of my **professional AI portfolio**. It is designed for a
learning journey in which I may study several courses at the same time,
move between topics during the week, and turn knowledge into practical
projects.

The goal is not simply to finish courses or collect certificates. The
goal is to develop real, demonstrable skills in Artificial Intelligence
and related fields.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. My objectives

At any time, this system should help me answer:

-   What have I studied?
-   What exactly have I learned?
-   Which courses, lessons, and topics have I completed?
-   How much focused study time have I invested?
-   What tests, exercises, and experiments have I completed?
-   What can I explain or implement independently?
-   What projects have I built?
-   What do I still need to learn or practise?
-   What evidence supports each skill level?
-   What can I confidently show to a supervisor, collaborator, or
    potential employer?

### Outcomes I am working towards

1.  **Learning archive** --- a detailed record of courses, sessions,
    notes, tests, mistakes, questions, and reflections.
2.  **Skills portfolio** --- practical evidence of what I can explain,
    implement, and apply.
3.  **Professional portfolio** --- selected, polished public projects
    that demonstrate my abilities.
4.  **Recognised achievements** --- completed courses, certificates,
    milestones, and project demonstrations.

## 3. How I organise my learning

I can study several courses at the same time. Therefore, I organise
learning in two connected ways:

-   **Learning areas** organise knowledge by subject.
-   **Courses and learning resources** organise the material I am
    following.
-   **Study sessions** record what I actually did on a particular day.
-   **Skills** record what I can demonstrate, regardless of which course
    taught it.
-   **Projects** turn knowledge into practical outcomes.

A course is not the same as a skill: completing a course does not
automatically mean I have mastered everything in it.

### Suggested learning areas

-   Python and programming tools
-   Mathematics and foundations for AI
-   Data analysis and data science
-   Machine Learning
-   Deep Learning
-   Computer Vision
-   Natural Language Processing
-   Generative AI and Large Language Models
-   RAG and AI agents
-   Research methods and technical communication

I can add, rename, or remove areas as my interests evolve.

## 4. Course tracking

Every course or learning resource should have its own record, even when
several courses are active at once.

For each course, record:

  Field                   What to record
  ----------------------- ---------------------------------------------------
  Course title            Exact course or resource name
  Platform                Coursera, edX, YouTube, book, documentation, etc.
  Instructor / provider   Name when relevant
  Learning area           Machine Learning, Deep Learning, etc.
  Course URL              Link to the resource
  Start date              Date I actually started
  Target completion       My intended finish date
  Status                  Not started, In progress, Paused, Completed
  Total duration          Published course duration, if known
  Actual study time       Time recorded in my sessions
  Progress                Modules or lessons completed / total
  Assessment              Quiz, test, assignment, or final assessment
  Certificate             Link or location when earned
  Main outcome            What I can demonstrate after studying it

### Course status definitions

-   **Not started:** Added to my learning plan, but no session
    completed.
-   **In progress:** I am actively studying it.
-   **Paused:** Temporarily inactive; it remains in my records.
-   **Completed:** I have finished the planned course requirements.
-   **Archived:** No longer active or relevant, but retained for
    history.

A course can be marked completed while some of its related skills remain
at a beginner or practice level.

## 6. Knowledge notes: understand, don't just collect

For important concepts, create notes in my own words.

A useful concept note includes:

1.  **Definition:** What is it?
2.  **Purpose:** Why do we use it?
3.  **How it works:** Explain the underlying idea.
4.  **Example:** Give a simple example.
5.  **Implementation:** Can I code it or use it?
6.  **Limitations:** When might it fail or be unsuitable?
7.  **Connections:** How does it relate to other concepts?
8.  **My explanation:** Can I explain it without copying the source?

Whenever possible, add a small code example, diagram, experiment, or
comparison.

### Rules for skill progression

-   Watching a video alone is not evidence of mastery.
-   Completing a course does not automatically increase every related
    skill.
-   A test score is useful evidence, but it is not the only evidence.
-   I should be able to explain the concept in my own words.
-   I should retain links to exercises, code, experiments, or projects.
-   If I cannot reproduce or explain something later, I should review
    it.
-   Skill levels can be reassessed; they are not permanent labels.

## 10. Weekly review

At the end of each week, review the full learning picture, including all
active courses.

### Weekly review questions

**Activity** - How many study sessions did I complete? - How many
focused hours did I invest? - Which courses did I work on? - Did I make
progress across several courses or concentrate on one?

**Learning** - What new concepts did I understand? - What can I explain
without assistance? - What did I practise or implement? - What tests or
assessments did I complete?

**Evidence** - What new code, exercise, note, or project can I show? -
Which skills have enough evidence to reassess? - What remains only
theoretical?

**Reflection** - What was difficult? - What mistakes did I make? - What
should I review? - What can I do now that I could not do last week?

**Next week** - Choose 1--3 realistic priorities. - Identify the next
action for each active course. - Reserve time for practical work, not
only course consumption. - Pause or archive courses that no longer
support my goals.

## 11. Dashboard: see all my active courses together

I want a visual overview because I may start multiple courses and study
different ones on different days.

The dashboard should let me see:

-   All active courses in one place
-   Course status
-   Learning area
-   Start date and target date
-   Progress
-   Last study date
-   Next action
-   Weekly study activity
-   Completed and paused courses

The recommended tool for this is **GitHub Projects**.

### Suggested GitHub Projects views

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  View                                Purpose
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Board (Kanban)                      Move courses between Not started,
                                      In progress, Paused, and Completed

  Table                               Compare all courses and their
                                      dates, progress, and learning areas

  Roadmap                             Visualise target completion dates
                                      and overlapping courses
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Suggested project fields

  Field                Type / example
  -------------------- ------------------------------------------------
  Course               Item title
  Status               Not started / In progress / Paused / Completed
  Learning area        Machine Learning / Deep Learning / etc.
  Priority             High / Medium / Low
  Start date           Date
  Target date          Date
  Progress             Percentage or completed modules
  Actual study hours   Number
  Last studied         Date
  Next action          Short text

**Recommended approach:** Create one GitHub Project named
`AI Learning Dashboard`. Add one item for each course. Use the board to
manage course status and the table to inspect details. Link each item to
its course record in this repository.

For a course with many lessons, use separate GitHub Issues or checklist
tasks for the lessons and assignments. Keep the main dashboard focused
on courses rather than filling it with every small activity.

GitHub Projects is a planning and visualisation tool; it will not
automatically calculate all learning statistics from Markdown files.
Update the fields manually at first. Automation or a custom dashboard
can be added later.

## 13. My working principles

1.  **Record consistently:** Every meaningful learning session should
    leave a record.
2.  **Learn actively:** Balance videos and reading with exercises,
    coding, and explanation.
3.  **Track honestly:** Never confuse time spent with knowledge
    acquired.
4.  **Collect evidence:** Link claims about skills to actual work.
5.  **Allow multiple active courses:** I can study different courses
    during the same week without losing track.
6.  **Review regularly:** Weekly reflection turns records into
    decisions.
7.  **Build continuously:** Convert learning into small implementations
    and larger projects.
8.  **Protect private information:** Keep the personal archive private
    and publish selected work intentionally.
9.  **Improve the system gradually:** The system should support
    learning, not become another complicated project that prevents it.

## 14. My definition of success

Success is not the number of courses I start or the number of hours I
spend watching lessons.

Success means that I can look back and clearly demonstrate:

-   What I studied.
-   What I understood.
-   What I practised.
-   What I can implement independently.
-   What I built.
-   What I can explain and defend.
-   Which certificates and achievements I earned.
-   How my abilities developed over time.

**My goal: turn learning into knowledge, knowledge into skills, and
skills into real projects and professional opportunities.**
